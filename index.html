Prologue

Every brochure in Hyderabad tells the same story

Saturday, 11 a.m., on the road to Kokapet. A couple in their thirties visits three villa launches in one morning. Each one has a pool, a gym, a jogging track, a banquet hall. The elevations are all white boxes with grey cladding. By lunch they can't remember which villa was which.

That is the market we are entering. We studied 27 villa types in Hyderabad gated communities, from Mokila and Tellapur to Kokapet, Narsingi and Manchirevula. They sell between 4 and 12 villas per acre, at prices from about ₹3.5 Cr for a 200 sq yd villa to ₹18 Cr and more for the largest plots. Almost all of them offer the same list of amenities.

The opportunity is simple to say and hard to copy: design the life, not the list. The chapters that follow start inside one villa and move outward, through the street, the landscape and the clubhouse, then to cost, and finish with what only Ankura will do.

Chapter one

The villa: four homes, four families

We start inside the home. All four sizes are planned to Vastu, in east-facing and west-facing versions. In the east-facing plans the road is on the east and the entrance, pooja and verandah sit in the north-east; the kitchen is in the south-east, and the master bedroom is in the south-west. The centre of every house, the brahmasthan, stays open to the sky with a tulsi plant. That open centre is also our design signature.

Every plan also follows the Ankura brief: a ground-floor bedroom for parents, a first-floor family lounge, walk-in wardrobes, a pooja room, wet and dry kitchens where the plot allows, and laundry and a gym at the top.

One Vastu template, four sizes

The same nine zones in every plan

  • North-east: main entrance, pooja room and an open verandah, with a meditation room and balcony above. No toilet, kitchen or septic tank here. The underground sump goes in this corner.
  • North: the living room, with the family lounge above it. There are no bedrooms in the north.
  • South-east: the kitchen, laid out so the cook faces east. The electric meter also goes here.
  • South and south-west: the stair and lift, the parents' bedroom and the master bedroom with wardrobes. The overhead tank sits above the south-west.
  • West and north-west: the dining room, toilets, utility and the guest and children's bedrooms.
  • Centre: a court open to the sky, with light, air and a tulsi plant.
  • North-west, at the front: the main entrance in the northern half of the west front, with a generous foyer and verandah, and no toilet beside the living room. The children's bedroom is above.
  • West, at the front: the car porch (the office in Size 4), with baths and a walk-in above.
  • North-east, at the back: the pooja room and a study opening onto a garden deck, with a meditation room and balcony above. The sump goes here, and nothing heavy.
  • North: the living room, with the family lounge above it. There are no bedrooms in the north.
  • East: the dining room next to the kitchen, with a powder room and the utility or staff room, and bed 3 above.
  • South-east, south and south-west: the kitchen (cook facing east), the stair and lift, and the parents' bedroom on the ground floor with its own attached bath on the south side. The master bedroom is above, and the overhead tank sits above the south-west.
  • Centre: the same open court with a tulsi plant.

Chapter two

The community: the street is the living room

4:30 p.m. School buses stop at the clubhouse by the gate, not at the doors. Children cycle home along a tree-lined green spine. No delivery van enters their lane. The vans stop at a parcel room at the gate. Grandparents sit on stone benches made from rocks found on the site.

Most Hyderabad communities are planned as plots and roads, with the leftover land called "landscape". We flip that. The green spine and clubhouse are drawn first, and the villas are arranged around them. The same rules work at three densities, so one idea can become a boutique estate or a larger, more affordable community.

1

Clubhouse at the gate

Visitors, cabs, school buses and deliveries stop here. Inner lanes stay quiet and safe.

2

One green spine

A tree-lined avenue runs from the gate to every lane. It is the main walking and cycling route.

3

Short, dead-end lanes

No through traffic. Each lane becomes a shared play street for its 8 to 18 homes.

4

Tree belt all around

A 20 ft planted edge gives privacy, shade and dust control, and room for a mini-forest.

5

Mixed sizes, one family

Bigger and smaller villas share lanes, so parents can buy a villa near their children's.

Chapter three

Landscape: Deccan, not Dubai

June, the first heavy rain. Water runs off the roofs, along planted channels beside each lane, and down into a stone step-well at the centre. By evening the step-well is a third full, and families sit on its steps to watch it fill. In April it was an amphitheatre.

Hyderabad sits on the granite of the Deccan Plateau. It has hot summers, sudden floods and falling groundwater. Our landscape should work with that climate and look like this place, instead of copying a lawn-and-palm resort.

01

Keep the rocks

Survey the site's granite boulders before levelling and keep the best ones as play rocks, seating and landmarks.

Outcome: a landmark at almost no cost, and a look no other city can copy.

02

A step-well at the heart

A stepped stone tank collects rainwater and doubles as an amphitheatre, following Telangana's own step-well tradition.

Outcome: water recharge, an event space, and a story for the brochure.

03

Planted channels instead of drains

Shallow planted channels (bioswales) along each lane slow storm water and soak it into the ground, with recharge pits at every junction.

Outcome: lower flood risk, more groundwater, less tanker water.

04

Mini-forest edge

Dense native planting (Miyawaki style) in the 20 ft boundary belt: neem, pongamia, tamarind and other local species.

Outcome: a cooler, quieter, dust-free edge that matures within a few years.

05

Food forest and farm

Orchards and allotment beds that residents can rent, supplying the clubhouse café.

Outcome: weekend activity for families, and the brief's orchards made useful.

06

Shade-first streets

A canopy tree every 20–25 ft, light-coloured paving and pergolas over bus stops and benches.

Outcome: lanes you can walk in May.

07

Night-sky lighting

Low, warm bollard lights and no glare. Some lanes stay dark enough for stars and fireflies.

Outcome: a calm, resort-like feel after sunset.

08

Healing and sensory garden

Level paths, handrails, fragrant plants and shaded seating for older residents and young children.

Outcome: three generations use the garden at the same time.

Chapter four

Amenities you feel with all five senses

5:45 a.m. Before the city wakes, a grandmother walks barefoot from grass to pebbles to cool mud. Parijat flowers fell overnight and the path smells of them. A Palapitta flashes blue from the bird hide. Her grandson is already at the food forest, checking whether the sitaphal is ripe.

Every Hyderabad community already has the usual list: pool, gym, courts, banquet hall. We will match it, but it won't sell our homes. Our idea is to move half of the amenities outdoors and design each around a human sense, so that people meet nature every day, not just on weekends.

The five-senses trail

One 775 m loop, twenty stops

The trail runs inside the 20 ft tree belt around the whole community. It takes about 10 minutes to walk one loop. Each side is themed around a sense, and the green spine and clubhouse carry the rest. Numbers on the map match the stops listed below.

Eight ways to cut cost without looking cheaper

01

One structural grid

All four sizes share the same column grid and standard window sizes, so formwork, steel and glazing repeat across the project.

02

Honest materials

Exposed brick, jaali and local granite remove the plaster, paint and cladding layers, which are a recurring cost.

03

Rat-trap bond walls

A hollow brick bond (popularised by Laurie Baker) uses fewer bricks and less mortar, and insulates better.

04

Filler slabs

Clay tiles or pots replace concrete in the part of a slab that carries no load, so the slab uses less concrete and steel.

05

Stacked wet cores

Kitchens and baths sit above each other, which shortens pipe runs and simplifies maintenance.

06

Shared walls at high density

In Cluster layouts, twin villas share one wall, saving a wall and a side setback per pair.

07

Site stone, precast edges

Rock found on site becomes compound walls and paving. Drains and kerbs are precast.

08

Bulk buying

One specification for all villas lets us buy tiles, sanitaryware, doors and lifts at volume prices.

Savings vary by site and contractor. Ask our structural engineer and quantity surveyor to price ideas 03, 04 and 06 against a conventional build before we commit to them.

Chapter six

The Ankura signature: ten things only we will do

Any developer can copy an amenity list. These ten ideas are harder to copy because they are built into the plan, the land and the way the community runs. Together they are the answer to "why Ankura?"

  1. Vastu with sky inside

    Every size, from 200 to 900 sq yd and east- or west-facing, follows Vastu, and its centre (the brahmasthan) is a court open to the sky.

  2. Parents' villa beside children's villa

    Size 1 and Size 3 villas share lanes, so families can buy two homes a few steps apart.

  3. Clubhouse at the gate

    Cars, cabs and deliveries stop at the edge. Inner lanes belong to children and walkers.

  4. The step-well heart

    A water-positive community centred on a stone step-well that also works as an amphitheatre.

  5. A five-senses trail

    A 775 m nature loop with twenty stops for sight, sound, touch, smell and taste, built around the site's own Deccan rocks.

  6. A Hyderabad design language

    Brick, jaali and stone, drawn from Deccan heritage and made modern. The screens shade the house as well as decorate it.

  7. A flexible top floor

    The terrace floor can be an office, gym, studio or rental suite, with a lift shaft from day one.

  8. Solar and EV in every home

    Rooftop solar, an EV point at each villa and a microgrid for common areas.

  9. Service like a resort

    Concierge app, parcel and cold lockers, car detailing, home maintenance and a pet spa.

  10. Designed for ageing well

    A ground-floor parents' bedroom, a lift, anti-skid floors, level paths and a health room.
